What we've done

Project work so far.

Our first chapter started in West Shore classrooms, where students taught lagoon lessons, ran nitrogen tests, and turned what they learned into posters, newsletters, and ordinance outreach.

So far, we have:

  • Taught lagoon lessons at 2 elementary schools
  • Led nitrogen testing and runoff demonstrations
  • Reached 200+ students through Project: Save Our Lagoon
  • Won 3rd place internationally at Future Problem Solving
  • Launched Project: BLACKOUT for Brevard's June 1–September 30 fertilizer ordinance

Mission

Why we started this.

The Lagoon Legacy Project grew out of Project: Save Our Lagoon, a student-led Future Problem Solving project started by West Shore students after seeing how little many local residents knew about the Indian River Lagoon's decline. After presenting in classrooms and watching younger students connect with the issue, we wanted the work to continue beyond one competition season.

Our early classroom work was supported through Labs 4 Learning and developed through Future Problem Solving's Community Problem Solving program. The first chapter is in Brevard County, where Project: Save Our Lagoon and Project: BLACKOUT keep that work local and practical.
